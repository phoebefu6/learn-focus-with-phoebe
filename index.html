Learn Focus with Phoebe

Attention is not a span you were issued; it is three jobs that tire, wander and recover, and the setting around you decides much of how they go. Six sessions on protecting and training it: what attention is and the goldfish number that is not, where the mind goes when it leaves the task, why focus fades over an hour and why the reason is still argued over, a bench that measures your own focus with and without chat banners, the room and the phone, and a focus block you can keep for two weeks. One constructed running case, a data engineer in an open-plan office, and research claims that each carry their status.

The six sessions 🧭 · from what attention is to a block you keep

The running case is Tomas, a constructed data engineer in an open-plan office, team chat open all day, desk facing the walkway to the kitchen. His best work needs forty uninterrupted minutes he rarely gets. Tomas and everyone around him are invented for teaching, and every page says so.

Not medical advice. These sessions are about everyday focus at work. They do not diagnose or treat anything. If trouble concentrating is persistent, affects your work or life, or comes with low mood, poor sleep or memory problems, talk to a doctor.

🔦 Session 1 · foundations

What attention is, and what it is not

Alerting, orienting and executive control: three jobs, not one span. The goldfish statistic traced to a source nobody could find, and an attention diary for one working morning.

▶ Start here 🎈 Session 2 · foundations

When the mind leaves the task

Minds wandered in 46.9 percent of 2,250 people's sampled moments. Where they go, how it feels, its costs and its uses, and how to catch it from the outside.

Why focus fades over an hour

The vigilance decrement, three rival explanations, a famous resource theory that failed a 23-lab replication, and attention restoration theory with its debate.

time on task ⏱️ Session 4 · bench night

The attention bench

A go/no-go task timed on you: baseline, chat banners, a break, banners muted. Then a modelled pool with an anti-lever and a break button that makes the measure fail.

★ the bench 📵 Session 5 · core

Designing the room and the phone

The phone-on-the-desk finding and the replication that did not find it, a week with notifications off, and a desk and chat setup that defends a block.

environment design 🧱 Session 6 · deeper

A focus block you can keep

Deep work as a practice rather than a finding, scheduled breaks against self-chosen ones, and a two-week experiment with a stop rule written in advance.

What is honest here 🧾

Attention advice travels with famous numbers, and several do not survive a look at their source. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Your reaction time, variability and errors on the benchMeasured on you in your browser, kept in this browser only, never sent anywhere; one sitting, not a diagnosis
Drift over 3, 10 and 20 minutes, the banner cost, the breakModelled on 200 synthetic participants by a rule printed on the widget; only directions are claimed
"Your attention span is shorter than a goldfish's"Untraceable. A marketing report credited a website whose listed sources had no record of the figures. Session 1 traces it
"A phone on the desk drains your brain"Contested. A 2017 finding that a pre-registered direct replication did not find. Session 5 teaches the disagreement
"Willpower is a resource that runs out"Failed a large replication: 23 labs, 2,141 people, an effect near zero. Session 3
"Nature restores attention"Some support, with uncertainty about which aspect of attention and small, varied studies. Session 3
